This is way better than I remember it being.

Oppenheimer is huge, the scale and size of this movie is just insane for what it is, this is a dialogue heavy film, it’s really this piece on morality, guilt, legacy, and politics, the epic qualities here would feel out of place if done by any other filmmaker, the glamour of this is fit for an action movie or something of the sort, but Nolan knows how to depict this story and the importance of it, we live in a post Oppenheimer world, our lives dictated by an iron fist, the threat of global destruction. As disorienting as this film can come across, with scientific jargon and a major concern placed on the threat of communism and government interest, this is a film, like most things in the world, dictated by emotion and individual values. The most compelling aspect of this is Oppenheimer’s personal guilt towards his creation, in his optimism he felt that the human race would strive under the conditions that war would be unthinkable if mass destruction was a possibility, but as soon as the bombs leave his grasp he knows that he has sent them beyond his control and he is now guilty in their inevitable use. Pairing the story of the creation of the atomic bomb alongside the courtroom drama parts of this which depict Oppenheimer’s political and social besmirchment perfectly highlights the emotional drivers of this story, Oppenheimers theoretical ideal coming at an impasse, represented through Strauss’ own pride and ego as he attempts to destroy Oppenheimers image. The two halves of this paint a picture of humanity’s affinity to destruction, we’d rather destroy ourselves than harm our own ego and pride, the final scene with Oppenheimer and Einstein is just a perfect conclusion. This movie has a lot to say but at its core I think this is a very cautionary film about our own ability to engineer our own demise.

This looks fantastic, I don’t think it is as consistently awe inspiring as Dunkirk but again the scale and ideas on display here is just something else. The suspension of reality in much of this film is amazing, especially when we’re heavily influenced by Oppenheimer’s own perspective, how this conveys his guilt or genius through unbelievable effect work, textured and dynamic camera movements, this film packs a punch. Ludwig Göransson is going to be a legend in film compositions, this is the modern day Hans Zimmer or John Williams, he is a genius and his score just slams through the whole film.

Cillian Murphy embodies this person, he is an incredible actor but this performance in particular feels like such a departure from what I’m used to from him, his movements and pattern of speech, everything he does just helps me lose him in this character. Everyone here is wonderful, this is a huge cast so I doubt I’d ever be able to mention all the little stuff I love, I think Robert Downey Junior is fantastic here, I want him to do more stuff like this so much, he is just impeccable. Emily Blunt is fantastic as well, her high class American accent is hilarious but totally believable, she really shines in the third act.

This movie is long and huge but is somehow one of the most energetic things I’ve ever watched, I don’t know how this works so well.
