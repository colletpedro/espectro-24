# Rotulagem manual (validação) — o bullet tem apoio nesta review?

São 40 pares NOVOS (nenhum da rotulagem anterior). Em cada um, um TEMA (o bullet da síntese, com a descrição que aparece em "Aprofundar") e UMA review do mesmo grupo.

> **Esta review sustenta o bullet?**

- **SIM** se a review conta como apoio para o bullet, NO MESMO SENTIDO. Apoio implícito conta, desde que sem ambiguidade. Review em outra língua conta.
- **NÃO** se a review fala do mesmo assunto com o juízo oposto (ex.: o bullet critica as atuações e a review as elogia).
- **NÃO** se a review atribui a opinião a outros e discorda dela (ex.: "dizem que é lento, mas não achei").
- **NÃO** se a review é ambivalente demais para servir de prova.
- **Bullet composto:** SIM se a review apoia o NÚCLEO do bullet, que é o título; NÃO se ela apoia só uma parte lateral (um detalhe da descrição, ou uma das ideias secundárias do título).
- **Na dúvida, NÃO.**
- Não julgue se o bullet é verdadeiro sobre o filme: julgue só se ESTA review o sustenta. Ignore quantas pessoas a descrição diz que concordam.

Responda em `rotulos_dono.csv` (mesma pasta), uma linha por par: `sim` ou `não`. Não deixe em branco — par sem rótulo trava a validação.

Because honestly, for most of this movie, all I could think was: why the fuck is this man incapable of respecting his wife’s boundaries? From the very beginning, Mother is uncomfortable with these people invading her home. This is their house. Her safe space. The place she has been rebuilding and caring for. And then the movie just keeps escalating. The guests become increasingly obsessive. The worship becomes increasingly disturbing. The house turns into complete chaos. And somehow, through all of this, Him continues to let them in. At some point I genuinely felt like I was watching a cult form around this man. People aren’t simply admiring him anymore—they’re worshipping him. They’re willing to destroy everything around them for him. And Mother? She is the one who actually creates. The most disturbing thing to me is that the people who worship Him don’t even truly care about what they destroy. They can adore him while simultaneously destroying the very thing that makes his creation possible. And he allows it because their love and worship matter more to him than protecting Mother. Which brings me to the ending, where I just sat there thinking: What the actual fuck? Mother gives everything. She is destroyed. The house is destroyed. The cycle ends in complete devastation. And Him? He survives. He takes what remains and starts again. So we’re essentially watching a cycle where Mother is repeatedly created, used, worshipped only for what she can give, destroyed by the people who claim to love the Creator, and then replaced by another Mother so the cycle can continue. I understand that this is supposed to represent something much larger. I understand the biblical parallels. Maybe there are dozens of things I could analyze about this movie. But as an actual viewing experience? I was miserable. I didn’t feel scared. I didn’t feel emotionally connected. I felt increasingly irritated at a man who couldn’t respect his wife’s privacy, increasingly uncomfortable watching a group of people turn into a cult around him, and increasingly horrified by the realization that nobody seemed to care about Mother as a person. One & half stars. I understand you, mother!. I just don’t like you. 💀
